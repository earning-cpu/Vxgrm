# Storyboard Analysis — 3 Concepts, 1 Approved

**Project:** Hindi horror short for YouTube Shorts (9:16, vertical, 1080×1920)
**Brief:** trendy / viral edit style · based on a real story · Hindi narration
**Decision:** Storyboard 01 approved for production. Boards 02 & 03 held as backups.

## Why Board 01 wins

**1. The hook is a sound, not a sentence.**
“पुणे का ये किला... आज भी एक आवाज़ सुनता है।” A fort that *still hears a voice* is an
open loop the viewer cannot close without watching. Boards 02 and 03 both open with a
statement of fact; Board 01 opens with an unresolved sensation.

**2. It is verifiable history, not folklore.**
Narayanrao Peshwa (b. 10 Aug 1755) was assassinated inside Shaniwar Wada on **30 August
1773**, aged 18. The order “नारायणराव ला धरा” (*seize Narayanrao*) was altered to
“**मारा**” (*slay*). His cry — “काका, मला वाचवा” (*uncle, save me*) — is recorded in
contemporary Maratha court accounts. The interior was destroyed by a **seven-day fire on
27 February 1828**. Every date in the film can be checked.

That matters commercially: “real story” claims that collapse in the comments kill a
channel's credibility. Board 01 survives a fact-check.

**3. It has a protagonist the viewer can lose.**
An 18-year-old hunted through his own home by his uncle's men. Boards 02 and 03 are
about *places* and *rules* — powerful, but nobody to root for. Loss is what makes horror
land, and loss needs a face.

**4. The visual range is built in.**
Ten distinct plates were produced from the board: fort gate, corridor, quill on parchment,
torched assassins, the running boy, the blood-trail courtyard, the river ghats, the 1828
inferno, the ruins today, and the black doorway. Board 02's six panels are all desert;
Board 03's are all one street. Board 01 changes world every 4–7 seconds, which is exactly
the retention rhythm Shorts rewards.

**5. The phrase is already culturally loaded.**
“काका मला वाचवा” is known across Maharashtra and the Hindi belt. Recognition in the first
ten seconds is free reach — it earns the “I know this story” comment that drives shares.

## Why the other two were not selected

**Board 02 — कुलधरा.** Genuinely eerie, and the “entry prohibited after sunset” detail is
a strong closer. But the disappearance is *legend-shaped*: the village was abandoned, yet
the “one night, 1,500 people” version has no contemporary record. It also has no
protagonist. Held as a backup — it would work as a slower, moodier piece.

**Board 03 — नाले बा.** The best pure *concept* of the three — second-person dread
(“if you hear your name, don't answer”) is perfect for comment-bait and “tag someone.”
It loses on two counts: it is folklore with no single documented event, and its visual
world is one street and one door. At 60 seconds it would repeat itself. The script is
preserved for a future episode.

## Production notes from the approved board

- **23 shots** across 7 acts, mapped 1:1 from the 10 board panels (extra shots are
  alternate crops/zooms of the same plates — normal practice, and it keeps the grade
  consistent).
- **Type system:** Noto Serif Devanagari Bold for the horror beats (काका मला वाचवा,
  धर/मार), Noto Sans Devanagari Bold for narration, Anton for Latin numerals (1773, 1828).
  Highlighted words sit on a dark marker box with a red accent bar so they stay legible
  over torchlight, parchment and moonlit stone.
- **Safe zones:** all critical type sits between y = 280 px and y = 1480 px, clear of the
  Shorts UI (top ~220 px, bottom ~420 px, right ~120 px).
- **Sound:** the board's SFX column became the actual cue sheet — whoosh on whip pans,
  impact on flash cuts, heartbeat through the chase, riser into the cry, fire crackle in
  the 1828 act, whispers under the legend.
