## Description
On July 14, 1518, a woman in Strasbourg stepped into the street — and began to dance. She didn't stop for days. Then dozens joined. Then up to 400. The city council prescribed MORE dancing. Some chronicles claim fifteen dancers died in a single day. The cause is still officially unsolved.

⚠️ Historical note: the dancing plague of 1518 is documented in Strasbourg city council minutes, physician notes, cathedral sermons and contemporary chronicles. The participant count (50–400), the death toll (unknown; "15 per day" is a chronicle claim) and the cause remain disputed by historians. Leading theory: mass psychogenic illness (John Waller). Alternative: ergot poisoning. No verdict exists.
